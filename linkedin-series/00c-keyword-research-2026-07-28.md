## What the market is actually calling this work right now

- **Job market signal:** agentic AI job postings grew ~280% year-over-year to ~90,000 US listings
  in 2026; postings mentioning "agentic systems" jumped from 151 (2024) to 16,500+ (2025) — the
  sharpest skill-demand shift in Stanford's 2026 AI Index. Job platforms list titles like
  **"Agentic AI Engineer," "AI Agent Developer," "AI Infrastructure Engineer,"** and
  **"AI Platform Engineer"** alongside traditional "DevOps Engineer"/"SRE" titles.
- **Infrastructure-side skills named explicitly** in current hiring guides: Kubernetes/Docker
  orchestration, CI/CD pipelines, multi-agent system design, tool orchestration, responsible/
  governed deployment — i.e., almost exactly what these 8 posts already document.
- **Recognized discipline terms not yet used anywhere in your 8 posts, all genuinely true of this
  build:** **AgentOps** (operating/observing autonomous agents in production — Post 5 is AgentOps
  content, it just doesn't use the word), **AI Gateway** (the standard term for exactly what
  ContextForge is — a policy/security/observability chokepoint between agents and backend
  systems), **AI Governance** / **Responsible AI** (Post 7's whole subject), **Zero-Trust** /
  **Least-Privilege AI** (Post 4's whole subject, described but never named with the industry term).
- **A notable, fixable gap:** Post 6 is specifically about the **A2A (Agent-to-Agent) protocol**
  and names it correctly in the caption body, but **has no `#A2A` hashtag** — a direct, exact-match
  miss for anyone searching that term specifically.

## One caption-level tweak worth considering (not just a hashtag)

Hashtags help LinkedIn's own tagging, but recruiters' literal keyword searches and Google indexing
both weight actual sentence text more heavily. Post 1's caption currently describes the work
("a production AI gateway... federating multiple MCP servers... with AI agents that delegate work
to each other") without ever using the phrase **"agentic AI infrastructure"** — the exact,
highest-growth category term found in this research. One clause using that exact phrase in Post 1
(the post every reader starts from) would do more for search-matching than any hashtag:

> Proposed insertion, in the paragraph that currently starts "Under my own company, GourmandTech
> LLC...": *"...I've spent the past year designing, building, and operating **agentic AI
> infrastructure** from scratch: IBM ContextForge running on Azure Kubernetes Service..."*
> (small insertion of the bolded phrase into the existing sentence, nothing else changes)

## Outside the posts themselves — the higher-leverage lever

Worth flagging directly: your LinkedIn **profile headline and About section** carry far more

your headline/About/Skills sections, adding them there would matter more than anything in this
